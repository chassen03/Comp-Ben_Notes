So, what actually is Job Evaluation?

Put simply, it is a systematic process intended to provide a rational, orderly hierarchy of jobs based on their worth to the company.

The Reality Check: Is vs. Is Not

A complete review of a position and the work done by the position.

It does NOT consider the individual in the position or their performance.

Considers the contribution of the position to the overall organization.

It is NOT a measure of how well the person in the position is performing.

A measure to ensure internal equity—compensating relative contribution consistently.

It is NOT a measure of salary competitiveness outside the organization.

Evaluates contribution internal to the organization.

Does NOT care how other companies measure the contribution of similar positions.

A nuanced process involving several components and related factors.

It is NOT a simple manual with check boxes to instantly finalize a rating.

Involves ongoing consultation between the employee, supervisors, and HR.

It is NOT one person in HR making a final decision in a silo without consultation.

Why Does It Matter?

Building a culture of trust and fairness.

Fair & Transparent

Evaluates roles within established parameters. Because the process is consistent, employees and managers know exactly how decisions are made, drastically reducing pay disputes.

Talent & Succession

A clearly mapped job architecture optimizes talent management. If directors see there is no natural successor for a role, they can proactively put plans in place early.

Equitable & Legal

Avoids wage discrimination based on gender, race, or age. It enriches the talent pool, builds a positive culture, and keeps the organization out of court with a hefty legal bill.

Timing is Everything

When Should Job Evaluation Occur?

Significant Changes

When there are significant changes to duties and responsibilities that impact rating factors (e.g., taking on supervision when it wasn't a prior responsibility).

New Positions

If a position is relatively new or unrated. They are initially tentatively rated, but over time as the position becomes defined, an evaluation validates the true rating.

Substantive Evolution

While time alone isn't a reason, working in a role over an extended period often leads to substantive changes in duties, processes, and interactions.

What does NOT trigger a re-evaluation?

An increase in the volume or amount of work being performed is not a consideration for re-evaluation as long as the type of duties performed are still the same. Minor changes also do not qualify.

The Job Evaluation Journey

A structured, multi-step process involving the employee, management, and HR.

Phase 1: Preparation & Approval

Step 1

The employee reviews the duties of their position. If there is a significant change (or it's unrated), the employee completes the Job Fact Sheet (JFS).

Step 2

The employee submits the JFS to their supervisor or manager for review and signature. (The manager may recommend changes prior to signing).

Step 3

The signed JFS is then submitted to the dean/director for their review and final managerial signature.

Phase 2: Submission & Initial Review

Step 4

The employee submits the fully signed JFS (with org chart) to Human Resources (via my.mun.ca "Submit a Case" or in-person at MyHR). Note: Any reclassification action is effective the date it is received by MyHR.

Step 5

MyHR sends an email confirmation of receipt with the effective date. The JFS is forwarded to the appropriate HR advisor.

Step 6

The HR advisor conducts an initial review to ensure changes are genuinely significant compared to the previous JFS. If not, the employee is notified in writing.

Step 7

The HR advisor schedules the full review. This may take 6-12 months depending on volume. Important: Any substantial changes occurring during this waiting period are not considered; a subsequent JFS would be needed.

Phase 3: The Deep Dive

Step 8

The HR advisor schedules individual meetings with the employee, supervisor, and dean to clarify questions, provide examples, and fully explain the work of the position.

Step 9

The position is formally rated within Human Resources in consultation with the job design and evaluation consultant utilizing Memorial’s application of the Aiken Plan.

Step 10

A characterization of the position (an overview relating to the nine factors of the Aiken Plan) is prepared and submitted to the dean/director for review.

Phase 4: Results & Next Steps

Step 11

Once confirmed, ratings are finalized and results are released to the employee via a Notification of Results.

Step 12a (Success)

If the employee is satisfied that the key functions were accurately captured, any required changes to adjust title, job class, and/or salary are processed.

Step 12b (Appeals)

If unsatisfied, the employee must complete a Request for Appeal within 10 business days. They must first meet with their HR advisor/consultant to review how the Aiken Plan was applied. No salary or title changes are processed until the appeal is completed.

Is All Job Evaluation Equal?
Absolutely Not.

Non-Analytical

Example: Paired Ranking

Compares a "whole-job" to other "whole-jobs" without breaking the role down into smaller demands. Creates a simple highest-to-lowest hierarchy.

  • Inherently biased
  • No objective scoring system
  • Will NOT stand up in an equal pay trial

Analytical

Example: Point-Factor

A quantitative form of evaluation that breaks jobs down into smaller components using defined factors (like the Aiken Plan). Assigns points based on the relevant level required.

  • Highly consistent & objective
  • The most reliable way to assess equal value
  • Translates easily into grade structures
